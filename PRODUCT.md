# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Plain static HTML/CSS/JS served from GitHub Pages, with a Supabase Edge Function (Deno/TypeScript) for the CV assistant. No bundler or framework today. Confirmed 2026-10-12: the user is **open to introducing a build step or framework** when the work genuinely calls for it (componentisation, i18n, templating). Static-by-default is the incumbent state, not a prohibition.

## Users

Two readers in sequence, and the page must satisfy both:

1. **Recruiters and talent sourcers, scanning fast.** A short credibility check before deciding to forward the candidate. They need role fit, proof, and a CV they can take with them in seconds.
2. **Hiring managers and engineering/AI leads, reading closely.** They decide whether Marco can actually build and own production AI. They dig into the architecture page and judge technical depth and evidence of shipped work.

Recruiter first, manager second. Failing either reader fails the site.

## Product Purpose

A personal professional site for Marco Rohns that converts a cold visitor into a real hiring conversation, for full-time roles, projects and collaborations in AI engineering, AI product and AI adoption.

Success is confirmed as three outcomes, all of them real:

- a direct email arrives about a role, project or collaboration;
- the CV leaves the page and travels inside a company (PDF download, or `resume.md` picked up by a person or an AI agent);
- visitors actually use the AskCV assistant and come away convinced it is grounded — the assistant is itself a demonstration of the capability being claimed.

## Positioning

The combination, evidenced rather than asserted: hands-on LLM/product engineering **and** 4.5 years of enterprise customer success and AI adoption — plus a live, revenue-earning product (ManeMap, iOS + Android, built solo end to end) and a public AI assistant on this very site that is hardened, documented and evaluated in the open.

A neighbouring portfolio cannot truthfully copy it: the site's own assistant, its published threat model and its regression-eval documentation are the proof of the claim, not a description of it.

## Operating Context

- Visitors arrive cold — from LinkedIn, a job application, a recruiter's link or a forwarded CV — mostly on mobile, often with seconds of patience.
- The CV exists in three forms that must agree: `resume.md` (source of truth, also readable by AI agents), `Marco_Rohns_CV.pdf` (printable, generated by `scripts/build-cv.py`), and `supabase/functions/ask-cv/cv.ts` (generated by `scripts/sync-cv.mjs` and served to the model).
- AskCV is budget-bounded in production: 15 questions per visitor and 1,000 per UTC day. Design must assume the assistant can legitimately be unavailable or rate-limited.
- A separate deep-dive surface, `architecture.html`, exists for the technical reader; `legal.html` and `privacy.html` are German-law obligations (Impressum, privacy), not optional pages.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Hard constraint — zero third-party requests.** Strict Content-Security-Policy, self-hosted fonts, no analytics, no trackers, no CDN. The only reachable external origin is the Supabase function endpoint. Nothing in any future design may introduce an outbound third-party request.

Other confirmed facts:

- Live at https://marcorhs.github.io (GitHub Pages).
- Shipped capabilities: static portfolio, CV in three synchronised forms, the grounded AskCV assistant (answers from the CV, names its source section, says "not in CV" when unsupported), the architecture deep dive, and legal/privacy pages.
- Privacy posture is a product commitment, not an implementation detail: IPs are never stored (salted hash, 3-day deletion), questions and answers are deleted after 90 days, and the page states this to the visitor.
- `resume.md` remains the binding factual source for page claims. No invented metrics, employers, testimonials, dates or clients may appear anywhere on the site.
- **Bilingual DE/EN is a committed direction for the page itself** (confirmed 2026-10-12), not just for the assistant. Future work should keep copy, navigation, layouts and components translation-ready, and should not assume English-only line lengths or labels. The assistant already answers in EN/DE/ES.

## Product Principles

1. **Proof over assertion.** Every capability claim should be backed by something the visitor can open, read, run or interrogate — the assistant, the architecture page, the threat model, the live app.
2. **Two readers, one page.** The recruiter's 60-second scan and the engineering lead's deep read must both succeed without the page apologising to either.
3. **State the limits.** Naming what the system does not know, cannot prove, or might get wrong is the credibility mechanism, not a weakness to hide.
4. **Nothing leaves the perimeter.** No third-party request, no tracking, no data kept longer than stated — the site practises the trustworthiness it claims to engineer.
5. **One source of facts.** `resume.md` is the truth; the PDF, the assistant and the page all trace back to it and never drift from it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No external standard has been formally committed. Incumbent practice to preserve: skip links, labelled landmarks and navigation, `aria-live` on assistant output, visible focus handling, and `prefers-reduced-motion` support (five blocks in `style.css`). The bilingual DE/EN commitment also makes correct `lang` attributes and translation-tolerant layout an accessibility requirement, not only a copy one.
